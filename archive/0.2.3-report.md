### 1. Why one-sided absorb is narrow — and better reaction options (all 4 live in `REACTION`)

A fixed rule where `R` consumes `G` at zero cost means even thin `R` beats dense `G` everywhere — no defense, no positioning reward. It only makes gameplay sense as a **3-player cycle (`R>G, B>R, G>B`)** or a temporary paid stance.

The panel now exposes all four options under **REACTION** so you can test them side by side:

- **`mutual`** *(default — symmetric `1:1` burn)*: Border war of attrition. Drain scales with overlap area and front length — fighting two neighbors bleeds you while a walled-off third player grows. Leaves dark no-man's-land seams.
- **`majority`** *(Lanchester law: minority loses `m`, majority loses `m · lo/hi`)*: **Fair rules, emergent asymmetry.** Equal fronts trade `1:1`, but a `200` core vs `50` mist loses `4×` less. Dense heartlands defend cheaply; breaking an enemy line requires concentrating pressure first.
- **`convert`** *(mass-conserving assimilation: minority transfers `lo · q · |a−b|/(a+b)` into majority)*: **Strongest fit for cultural influence.** Zero black dead zones, vibrant glowing mixed seams at equal borders, quantum-exact total `R+G+B` mass conservation. Opening `mix` becomes a symmetric wager: if your border pressure is higher you convert their population into yours; if you spread too thin they convert yours.
- **`absorb`** *(one-sided `A>B` with clickable `R>G` ↔ `G>R` badges; defaults to cyclic `R>G, B>R, G>B`)*: Lets you test both single-pair absorb and 3-player rock-paper-scissors chase dynamics (forces using one rival as a buffer against your predator).

### 2. Player resource & emergent self-limits — game implementation variants

Right now unlimited sources reward spamming everywhere. Five ways the simulation itself limits the player without arbitrary caps:

1. **Shared supply flow (`Player supply` slider — implemented in `0.2.3` to test)**
   Each player has one per-frame injection flow divided across all their active sources (later optionally `+ k·√owned` for diminishing empire returns). Combined with the `255` cell ceiling and depletion, source count forms a natural **inverted-U sweet spot** (measured at `Player supply = 600/f, Depletion = 0.1/f`):
   - `1` source → `1,521` cells (`peak 255` — chokes on the single-cell ceiling, wastes supply)
   - `2–4` sources → **`3,292–3,475` cells** (`peak 150–255` — optimal reach + strong pressure)
   - `16` sources → `1,552` cells (`peak 37` — diluted, vulnerable to `majority`/`convert`)
   - `64` sources → `576` cells (`peak 9` — collapses into depletion)
2. **Territory-fed reserve (`reserve[p]`) + physics-driven frontier upkeep**
   Settled, pure interior cells trickle into `reserve[p]`; sources spend directly from `reserve[p]` as they inject `255 − ch`. Because peaceful saturated interior slows injection to near `0` while active war fronts rapidly drain `ch`, **frontier hubs automatically cost ~10–20× more than safe interior hubs** with zero special combat rules. Over-building causes empire-wide supply bankruptcy.
3. **Conserved population pool (`Total color limit` / Boyle's law)**
   Total gas mass *is* your resource (`pressure ≈ mass / area`). Grabbing empty land drops your pressure across every front at once, inviting invasion or conversion elsewhere.
4. **3-player diplomatic policy levers (pair openness vs zeal)**
   Turn per-pair `mix` (open borders / trade) and `react` (intolerance / assimilation) into player stances:
   - Open `mix` + low `react` generates bonus supply from `pairCells` overlap ("cultural exchange wealth"), at the risk of getting converted if your neighbor builds higher local density.
   - High `react` purges infiltration, but burns mass (`mutual`/`majority`) and destroys trade income — rewarding alliances against the leader.
5. **Discrete hub placement ("Liquid Go")**
   Cooldown- or turn-based placement/relocation of a small set of hubs plus diplomatic toggles: slow, readable gradients with deep geometric blocking and encirclement.

### 3. What comes next — dynamic source movement & spawn/despawn physics

Replacing raw static markers with lightweight hub entities (`hubs.js`, rasterized onto `Field.src` each tick) opens four strong directions (combinable):

- **Urban nucleation & siege collapse/flip *(recommended first)*:**
  - *Spawn*: a cell holding high pure density (`> T_spawn`) for `N` frames outside an inhibition radius `R_min` automatically founds a secondary town (which shares `playerFlow` — organic growth that can also overextend you).
  - *Despawn / capture*: if enemy pressure or conversion keeps a hub's neighborhood below `T_die` for `N` frames, the hub collapses or flips to the dominant rival — giving offensives concrete targets to encircle and flip.
- **Pressure-coupled hub drift:**
  - *Buoyant capitals*: foreign pressure pushes hubs back while own-territory gradients pull them toward their region's center (smooth Lloyd-like shifting as borders move).
  - *Nomad fronts*: hubs drift down `-∇ch[p]` toward empty space and stall where rival pressure balances them.
- **Shifting fertile wells (map geography):**
  Hotspots spawn, drift, peak, and fade across the map; holding majority color over a live well grants free injection or victory points, preventing late-game 3-way stalemates.
- **Mitosis & merge:**
  A saturated hub accumulating unused supply buds a daughter hub down the steepest escape gradient; crowded friendly hubs merge into a higher-ceiling capital.
