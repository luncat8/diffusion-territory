# 0.3.0 draft — gameplay economy, pair interaction variants, and dynamic sources

Design options for turning the three-color cultural gas simulation into a playable strategy loop
where limits arise from the field itself rather than hard action rules.

## 1. why static one-sided pair absorb is narrow, and better alternatives

A fixed one-sided pair (`R>G`: G shrinks, R pays nothing) makes R beat G unconditionally at any
density, removing defensive play. It works only as a symmetric 3-player cycle (`R>G, B>R, G>B` —
chasing fronts and forced buffer diplomacy) or as a temporary, paid stance.

Four reaction laws shipped in `0.2.3` (`reactMode`) cover the main design space:

- **mutual** (`1:1` burn) — border attrition. Cost scales with overlap area and front length;
  overextended tendrils and two-front wars bleed mass while a walled third player grows.
- **majority** (Lanchester `lo/hi` discount) — fair across players, asymmetric by local density.
  Compact, well-fed cores scrub thin infiltrators cheaply; breaking a line requires concentrating
  pressure above local parity.
- **convert** (mass-conserving flip toward local majority) — closest match to cultural assimilation.
  Leaves no black dead zones; opening a pair border becomes a symmetric wager (assimilate weaker
  neighbor gas, or get assimilated if spread too thin).
- **absorb** (directed `A>B`, default cyclic `R>G, B>R, G>B`) — ecological rock-paper-scissors;
  useful for rotating 3-player chase maps or asymmetric faction matchups.

## 2. player resource and emergent self-limits

Five compatible economy models built directly on existing field physics:

1. **Shared supply flow** (`playerFlow`, shipped in `0.2.3`) — fixed per-frame injection shared
   across all of a player's active sources (optionally `+ k * sqrt(owned)` for sublinear empire
   income). Few sources bottleneck against the `255` ceiling; too many dilute per-source pressure
   and lose to depletion and `majority`/`convert` fronts.
2. **Territory-fed reserve (`reserve[p]`)** — settled, pure interior cells trickle into a reserve;
   source injection spends directly from `reserve[p]`. Because `Source.inject` tops up `255 - ch`,
   peaceful saturated interior hubs draw near zero while contested frontier hubs drain reserve fast
   without special combat-cost rules. Overbuilding triggers automatic supply bankruptcy.
3. **Conserved population (`maxMass`, Boyle's law)** — total mass is the resource (`pressure = M/A`).
   Expanding into empty space drops pressure everywhere, weakening defense on other fronts.
4. **Diplomatic policy trade-offs (pairs as player levers)** — each player sets openness (`mix`)
   and zeal (`react`) toward each neighbor. Open + low react yields cultural exchange bonus (extra
   `playerFlow` from `pairCells`) but risks conversion; high react defends identity but burns mass
   and kills exchange income.
5. **Liquid Go (discrete hub placement)** — cooldown or turn-based placement/relocation of a small
   number of hubs plus stance toggles; easy to read, deep positional geometry.

## 3. next step — dynamic source movement and spawn/despawn physics

Represent sources as lightweight hub objects rasterized into `Field.src` each tick (`hubs.js`):

- **Nucleation & siege collapse** — sustained high pure density (`ch[p] > T_spawn` for `N` frames)
  outside an inhibition radius `R_min` spawns a secondary town (sharing `playerFlow`); a hub whose
  neighborhood flips or falls below `T_die` for `N` frames collapses or converts to the attacker.
- **Field-driven hub drift** — hubs move slowly under local pressure forces: either frontier drift
  (`-grad ch[p]` into empty space, halting against rival pressure) or buoyant recoil (pushed back by
  foreign pressure, settling near territory centroids like Lloyd relaxation).
- **Shifting fertile wells** — map hotspots spawn, drift, peak, and fade; holding majority density
  over a live well grants bonus injection or victory score, breaking late-game stalemates.
- **Mitosis & merge** — saturated hubs storing surplus flow bud a daughter hub down the steepest
  escape gradient; nearby friendly hubs coalesce into a higher-ceiling capital.
